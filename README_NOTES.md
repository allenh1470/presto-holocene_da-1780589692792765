Fennoscandia data-driven PReSto experiment. I am interested in investigating a ~1.5 ka glacial fluctuation recorded in Eastern Beringia and in some places in Fennoscandia. I made this repository and another 
(https://github.com/allenh1470/presto-holocene_da-17805890138156602) that focuses on Alaska-driven simulations. I was interested in seeing whether either region recorded a ~1.5 ka temperature transition that could help explain 
glacier expansion in their respective regions. The two regions have interesting teleconnections through the Arctic Oscillation and Aleutian Low-Icelandic Low dipole. This experiment also examined whether climate was driving 
temperature change in one region, with the other region reacting.

Both datasets focused only on lake sediments, inferred annual temperatures, and used Holocene DA.

Results: Around 1.5 ka, the Fennoscandian reconstruction shows no cooling, while the Alaskan reconstruction shows a general, very subtle cooling over the Arctic. After investigating a time series at the same latitude in both 
regions, the overall temperature trend over the last 5 ka or so is remarkably similar; however, both are flat, with little deviation from 0 °C. 

General temperature variability around this time was limited both in the Arctic and regionally according to both reconstructions. It was interesting to see how selecting a regional-only approach affects temperature 
estimates in other regions of the Arctic.
